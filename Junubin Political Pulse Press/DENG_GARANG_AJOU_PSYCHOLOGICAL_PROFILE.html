BEHIND the fiery emojis and menacing headlines of Junubin Political Pulse Press sits not a powerful spymaster or state-backed intelligence officer, but a vulnerable young man caught in a dangerous game of proxy war. To understand how to neutralize or control the subject, one must first dismantle his psychological illusion.

Deng Garang Ajou suffers from what forensic psychologists term The Digital Mask Syndrome. In the physical realm of Juba and Nairobi, he is powerless—a recent graduate without an institutional title, desperate for rent, and dependent on political crumbs. But behind the admin dashboard of a Facebook page, he experiences an intoxicating rush of god-like influence.

This artificial power creates an acute inferiority-superiority complex. While he trembles at the thought of encountering a junior National Security Service (NSS) officer in person, his digital persona attacks the First Lady, challenges presidential paternity, and claims to shake the pillars of J1.

The Two Faces

The most defining feature of his psyche is his split identity. On his verified personal profile, he presents the image of a humble, religious, and loyal party cadre. He congratulates senior ministers, seeks mentors, and bows to authority.

Yet on the Junubin page, the suppressed resentment breaks free. Here, he acts as an extortionist and attack dog. He does not attack out of ideological conviction; he attacks because someone paid him a stipend, or because he hopes the threat of an article will force his victims to buy his silence.

Because his malice is driven by financial survival rather than political conviction, his loyalty is completely transactional. Whoever pays his bill commands his pen.

His keyboard is loud only because his identity was hidden. Strip away his anonymity, and the attack dog immediately whimpers.

The Clout Addiction

Ajou has developed a profound addiction to social media metrics. He measures his personal worth by comment counts, shares, and reactions. When political posts fail to generate traction, he immediately resorts to posting sensational TikTok feuds (Nyandeng Bol, Akuac Wol) to maintain high dopamine flow.

This craving for attention makes him arrogant and careless. He convinced himself that he was a stealth genius, blinded by vanity to the point where he used the exact same writing mannerisms, punctuation quirks, and burner phone connections across all assets.

The Shadow of Fear

Despite his bold online persona, Ajou is driven by overwhelming fear. His greatest dread is physical detention. He understands the brutal reality of Juba's security organs, and the thought of being traced to a physical room sends him into rapid panic.

When confronted with irrefutable proof of his identity, individuals of his profile do not fight back. They delete accounts, hide SIM cards, flee their residences, or turn into state informants to save their own skin.
